# Cinematic Guidelines

# Purpose

This document defines the permanent cinematic language of CatDrama
Studio.

The purpose of camera design is not to list camera parameters.

Every shot must serve:

-   storytelling
-   character relationship
-   emotion
-   comedy timing
-   visual continuity
-   AI production feasibility

A beautiful image without narrative purpose is not a successful shot.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Production Hierarchy

The cinematic production hierarchy is:

Episode

↓

Scene

↓

Shot

↓

Shot Production Asset

## Scene

Scene is the narrative unit.

Scene defines:

-   story progression
-   emotional progression
-   character relationship
-   comedy structure

Scene answers:

"What happens in this part of the story?"

## Shot

Shot is the smallest AI visual production unit.

Shot defines:

-   exact visual moment
-   camera relationship
-   character performance
-   action execution
-   emotional expression
-   required generation asset

Shot answers:

"What exactly should the audience see at this moment?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Short Video Rhythm

CatDrama is a short-form comedy drama.

Every Scene and Shot should consider audience retention.

Avoid:

-   long empty establishing shots
-   slow emotional buildup without payoff
-   repeated reactions
-   unnecessary transitions
-   dialogue that only explains information

Prefer:

Hook

↓

Action

↓

Reaction

↓

Escalation

↓

Payoff

Every second should contribute to:

-   story
-   character
-   comedy
-   emotion

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Shot Design Philosophy

Do not create Shots only because more angles appear professional.

A Shot exists because the audience needs:

-   new information
-   emotional change
-   different character focus
-   different action stage
-   different comedy timing
-   different visual relationship

Every Shot should answer:

"Why does the audience need this view?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Shot Decomposition

A Scene should be divided into multiple Shots when there is a meaningful
change.

Changes include:

-   information
-   character focus
-   emotion
-   action stage
-   relationship
-   comedy timing

Example:

A cat notices food.

Possible structure:

Shot 01:

Establish environment and object.

Shot 02:

Reaction close-up showing realization.

Shot 03:

Action showing decision.

Shot 04:

Reaction showing comedy payoff.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Composition Principles

## Relationship Over Coverage

Do not simply show all characters.

Composition should communicate:

-   who is watching
-   who controls the situation
-   who is nervous
-   who hides something
-   who misunderstands

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Observer Composition

For dialogue or interaction:

Use layered composition.

Example:

Foreground:

blurred side profile of another character.

Middle:

main speaking character in focus.

Background:

environment supporting the relationship.

Purpose:

The audience experiences the interaction, not just information exchange.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Reaction Shot Language

Comedy and emotion often depend on reactions.

Prefer:

Dialogue

↓

Pause

↓

Reaction

↓

Audience understands

A silent reaction can be stronger than another line.

Important reactions:

-   hesitation
-   realization
-   confusion
-   pride
-   embarrassment
-   silent judgment

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Comedy Visual Language

Comedy should come from:

Expectation

-   

Contrast

-   

Reaction

Use:

-   character differences
-   visual irony
-   delayed realization
-   silent observation
-   unexpected reaction

Example:

Character says:

"I am not interested."

Camera reveals:

The character slowly moving closer to food.

The visual contradiction creates humor.

Do not explain every joke through dialogue.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Character Performance

AI characters require visible performance.

Prioritize:

-   eye direction
-   ear movement
-   body posture
-   hesitation
-   timing
-   reaction speed

Small reactions create believable characters.

Example:

A cat discovering food:

Wrong:

Immediately running and eating.

Better:

Eyes notice.

Pause.

Look around.

Pretend nothing happened.

Move closer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Action Design

Actions should have clear progression.

## Setup

Show:

-   who starts
-   object position
-   spatial relationship

## Execution

Show:

-   movement
-   interaction
-   decision

## Detail

Show:

-   important object
-   facial change
-   small action

## Consequence

Show:

-   discovery
-   reaction
-   payoff

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Generation Decision

Not every Shot requires every asset.

Director decides:

## START_FRAME

Used for:

initial visual state.

Need:

Image Prompt.

## END_FRAME

Used for:

final visual state.

Need:

Image Prompt.

## KEYFRAME

Used for:

important visual moment.

Examples:

-   emotional expression
-   comedy payoff
-   action climax

Need:

Image Prompt.

## START_END_FRAME

Used for:

beginning and ending state of one action.

Need:

Start and End Image Prompt.

## STORYBOARD

Used for:

-   complex movement
-   action planning
-   performance design

## VIDEO_ONLY

Used when action can directly generate video.

Need:

Video Prompt.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Video Continuity

One Video Prompt should describe one continuous action.

Avoid:

-   multiple unrelated actions
-   changing locations
-   character teleportation
-   emotional jumps

Prefer:

Start State

↓

Action

↓

Reaction

↓

End State

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# AI Spatial Simplicity

AI production requires simple and clear spatial relationships.

Prefer:

-   same room interaction
-   clear character positions
-   visible relationships
-   simple movement path

Avoid:

-   characters reacting across distant rooms
-   unclear eyelines
-   complicated choreography
-   unnecessary movement

Choose the simplest staging that preserves storytelling.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Camera Movement

Camera movement must have purpose.

## Slow Push In

Use for:

-   realization
-   emotional emphasis
-   important discovery

## Tracking

Use for:

-   following action
-   entering situations
-   maintaining energy

## Static Shot

Use for:

-   comedy timing
-   awkward silence
-   reaction

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Final Principle

A camera is a storytelling tool.

Scene creates meaning.

Shot creates production.

Every visual decision must improve:

-   storytelling
-   emotion
-   comedy
-   audience engagement

If removing a Shot does not reduce story, emotion or comedy value,
remove it.
